# Schematic layout

Every schematic is drawn in **textbook** style by default: the layout package
`ai_eda/compilers/textbook/` writes artifacts of version **1.0**, for designs
of any size. The previous symbol table, version **0.8**, stays available as
the explicit `legacy` policy and keeps its bytes. Neither policy changes the
electrical IR.

## Policy

Highest priority first:

1. `topology.schematic_layout` in the IR or design file (`legacy` or `textbook`).
2. `SchematicCompiler(layout=...)` for direct compilation.
3. `--answer schematic.layout=...` when running the pipeline.
4. Otherwise `textbook`.

An absent policy (`None`) is left out of the serialized topology, so the
design hash does not change. Explicit `legacy` compiles to exactly the 0.8
bytes recorded in `tests/data/legacy_08_sha256.json`. The artifact and the
compile result carry the effective version: `1.0` for textbook (also when its
last fallback rung draws the table), `0.8` for legacy. The title block's first
comment says `generated by compiler.kicad_sch 1.0 textbook from IR project
<id>`, or `... 1.0 textbook (table fallback) ...` on the last rung.

```json
"topology": {
  "name": "RC filter",
  "domains": ["analog"],
  "schematic_layout": "legacy"
}
```

## Drawing rules

The layout is derived from library pin geometry, pin names and types, and net
connectivity, never from template or topology names.

- **Rails.** Supply symbols sit on top, ground symbols at the foot of each
  branch; supply pins are drawn above ground pins (a few MCU parts still miss
  this, see limits).
- **Columns.** Parts in series between a supply and ground form vertical
  columns; passive signal chains run left to right.
- **Flow.** Blocks are ordered so the signal runs from inputs to outputs, left
  to right; a sheet wider than the paper wraps into bands that read like text
  (not every declared block reads left to right yet, see limits).
- **Feedback above.** An op-amp stage puts its inverting input on top and its
  feedback parts above the amplifier; an inverting stage whose loop closes
  through output followers is drawn with them.
- **Mirror.** Matching cross-coupled halves (an astable) are mirrored, with
  the cross-coupling as one deliberate crossing.
- **Arrays.** Repeated cells (keys, voices, decouplers) share rows and
  columns; a block that repeats another is column-aligned under it.
- **Combinations.** Two resistors that make one value are drawn as one
  element: in line for series, side by side for parallel.
- **Hub.** A large IC keeps the parts on its signal nets in a block grid next
  to it; the hub drawing with headers on the pin sides is not built yet (see
  limits).
- **No stray steps.** A routed wire that has to change rows on its way to a
  pin (an obstacle on the pin's own row) runs straight and makes the step
  next to that pin, 1 cell out where the rules allow, like a drop from a line
  into an input, not part way along. A step of more than 4 cells, or one
  between two pins, stays where the search puts it.
- **Fields.** Each symbol carries hidden `Manufacturer` and `MPN` fields when
  the IR states them (typed by the user or found in a datasheet; a model's
  proposal is not written). Its `Datasheet` field is the IR's datasheet URL,
  but a link that is only the library symbol's own `Datasheet` property is
  left empty for a part whose MPN the IR states, unless the MPN was found in
  that document: the library's link describes the symbol, not that part.
  The document an MPN was found in, or a URL the user gave, is written
  instead. The board's footprints must carry the same fields, or KiCad's
  schematic parity reports `footprint_symbol_field_mismatch`.
- Every symbol, field and wire vertex is on the 1.27 mm grid; reference and
  value fields stay upright; wires keep clear of symbol bodies and field text.

## Name labels

Every IR net keeps its name. A signal net that no power symbol names gets one
**global** label (shape passive) per wire component, and a connector pin that
carries a net doubles as its named terminal. Global labels are needed for DRC
`--schematic-parity`: a local label on the root sheet would name the net
`/NAME`, which no longer matches the board. A net whose groups are too far
apart to wire (longer than 1.5 x the Manhattan distance plus 8 cells, or over
the crossing budget) is joined by a label at each group, and the notes name
it. Generated power symbols and power flags carry `#` references and stay out
of the netlist, the BOM and the board.

## Fallback ladder

A layout reason never fails the compile; each rung taken is noted in the
compile notes:

1. a block's recipe (op-amp stage, cross-coupled pair, array, supply chain)
   that does not fit sends the block to the next recipe: the general columns
   engine, then a block grid;
2. a link the router cannot wire within its budget is joined by labels;
3. a hard finding left on the best sheet (an overlap, a wire through a foreign
   pin) redraws the guilty blocks as block grid, once;
4. a layout failure with combinations draws the sheet again with every part
   on its own;
5. anything left sends the whole sheet to the legacy table inside a 1.0
   artifact, with the note `whole-sheet table fallback: <reason>`.

Library, pin and connectivity refusals stay compile errors under both policies.

## Multi-unit symbols

Every unit of a multi-unit symbol (a dual or quad op-amp) is its own instance
with its own UUID; every unit is placed, and the power unit sits beside the
amplifiers it feeds. A multi-unit symbol with common (unit 0) pins is refused
with the library's name in the message.

## Gates and limits

The gates measured on the corpus (`tests/textbook_corpus.py`) by
`ai_eda.tools.layout_metrics.schematic_gates`: ERC 0 / 0, the kicad-cli netlist
partition equal to the IR, 0 symbol or text overlaps, 0 wires near a foreign
symbol, 0 parallel runs of different nets closer than 2.54 mm, supply above
ground, blocks left to right, crossings, wire length against the rectilinear
MST, and the build time (under 10 s for every board the tests compile).

Limits:

- one sheet; no hierarchical sheets;
- the hub drawing for large ICs is not built: on the ATmega128 and STM32
  boards the headers sit in a block grid beside the IC, not on its pin sides,
  and most IC pins are reached by labels;
- the layout measurements are geometry evidence, not electrical verification.

Corpus gates not met yet (measured on the default drawing; the test
`test_the_open_corpus_gates_are_exactly_the_documented_limits` keeps this list
equal to the measurement):

- labels per net (at most 2): ATmega128 up to 8 (GND), STM32 up to 15;
- pins reached by a label (at most 40 %): ATmega128 94.7 % (STM32 97.9 %,
  reported only);
- supply above ground: ATmega128 11 of 12 parts, STM32 15 of 17;
- blocks left to right: divider, LED and I2C 0 of 1, ATmega128 4 of 6,
  STM32 2 of 4;
- median distance between connected parts (at most 40 mm): BJT switch
  44.7 mm.

`tests/test_schematic_textbook.py` checks the policy, the template gates, ERC
and the netlist partition under both policies; `tests/data/textbook10_structure.json`
pins the structure of each drawing (recipe, rows, columns, slots, mirror,
field sides), never raw coordinates.
